# 🟣 Task 4 — Data Storytelling & Statistical Validation

### Objective

Synthesize the findings from the previous tasks into a compelling business story and validate a business hypothesis using statistical analysis.

### Business Question

> **Is average `Total_Sales` significantly different between male and female customers?**

### Hypotheses

**Null Hypothesis (H₀):**

There is no statistically significant difference in average `Total_Sales` between male and female customers.

**Alternative Hypothesis (H₁):**

There is a statistically significant difference in average `Total_Sales` between male and female customers.

### Statistical Test

**Welch's Independent Two-Sample t-test**

* Significance level: **α = 0.05**
* Confidence level: **95%**
* Two-tailed test

### Results

| Metric              |       Male |     Female |
| ------------------- | ---------: | ---------: |
| Sample Size         |        511 |        489 |
| Average Total Sales | 141,807.34 | 136,883.21 |

**T-statistic:** `0.6826`

**P-value:** `0.495011`

### Statistical Decision

```text
p-value = 0.495011

0.495011 > 0.05
```

Therefore:

### **Fail to Reject H₀**

There is insufficient statistical evidence to conclude that average `Total_Sales` differs significantly between male and female customers.

### Business Conclusion

Although the observed average sales differ between male and female customers, the statistical test does not provide sufficient evidence that the difference is statistically significant.

Therefore, **gender alone should not be used as a major factor for sales or marketing decisions**.

### Business Recommendations

* Prioritize strong-performing product categories.
* Focus regional strategies on high-performing markets.
* Use monthly sales patterns for campaign and inventory planning.
* Validate customer-segment assumptions statistically before making major business decisions.

Inventory allocation and promotional strategies can prioritize stronger-performing categories while reviewing underperforming product lines.
